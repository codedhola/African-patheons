Yorùbá òrìṣà of thunder and lightning

Ṣàngó of Ọ̀yọ́

A photoreal study of the thunder king in full regalia: a red aso-oke agbádá that hangs from his raised arm, a beaded crown with its veil, the double axe held high and the gourd rattle in his other hand.

What is sourced, what is imagined

Classical shrine art shows Ṣàngó through his devotees and emblems rather than as a portrait, so any face given to him is an interpretation. This list keeps the two apart.

From the sources

  • Red cloth with white squares for his ritual garments, made up here as agbádá, bùbá and ṣòkòtò. Wikipedia: Shango
  • Red and white beads in fours or sixes. Every strand here counts six red, then six white. Wikipedia: Shango
  • The oṣé raised overhead. Eyes of a Lagos Boy, Chazen
  • The làbà and the ṣẹ̀rẹ̀: a goat-leather bag embroidered with strips dyed red and yellow, carrying the axe and the seed-filled gourd. Michael Backman Ltd
  • Àbàjà marks, six lines a cheek. Wikipedia: Yoruba tribal marks
  • A conical beaded crown with a veil and a bird on top. High Museum of Art
  • A wide gown of strip-woven cloth, embroidered, sleeves bunched at the shoulders. Smarthistory

This study's choices

  • His face, build and beard. No source describes how Ṣàngó looks; he is drawn as a strong man in his thirties.
  • The crown's motifs. The zigzag lightning, the white axe emblem and the bird's shape are designed for this figure, not copied from one crown. The veil is cut short to show the face.
  • The embroidery. Spirals and an oṣé inside a teardrop panel, in white thread, in the general manner of agbádá neck embroidery.
  • The cloth's scale. 10.5 cm strips with two woven squares across each, each square with a red centre.
  • The pose and the storm. The raised arm follows the statue; the wet ground and distant lightning set the scene for the god of thunder.
  • The bag's colours. Its red body and the white on its flap and fringe are this study's; the source names red and yellow dyes.
  • Bangles and bracelets. Brass and beads at the wrists are decoration, not a sourced attribute.

How he was made

The body starts from MakeHuman's free base mesh, shaped and rigged, then posed bone by bone. The fists were closed around the handles by fitting the fingers to each grip.

The agbádá is one cloth pattern, a rectangle folded at the shoulders with a neck opening and sewn sides. It was draped on the body with the arms out, then simulated while the arms moved into the final pose, so every fold comes from the cloth hanging off the raised arm.

Beads, strands and strap were placed by small physics passes: the necklaces were dropped over the head and allowed to settle on the cloth, the crown's veil slides over the head and ears, and the strap was pulled taut over the shoulder.
