      Cloth, beads and marks

      The figures wear real Yoruba and Igbo textiles and regalia, drawn in code from published descriptions. Swatches show the patterns as they appear on the figures.

      How the figures were made

      • Every figure is built in your browser from simple shapes with three.js. Nothing is scanned or photographed, and no figure copies a particular artwork.
      • Under each god, What this figure wears labels every element. A source name means the detail is documented there; Our choice marks a gap we filled, such as a cap colour or a staff the sources name but never describe.
      • Heads are drawn a little larger than life so that faces, marks and headwear read at small sizes.
      • Colours used in Cuba and Brazil are labelled as such; Nigerian lineages keep their own bead codes.

      Respect notes

      • Olódùmarè and Chukwu have no images in tradition, so they appear only as light.
      • Many alụsị are understood as unseen forces, and Amadioha is said to have no human form at all. The Igbo figures are interpretations in Igbo dress, not cult images.
      • Yoruba shrine art shows Ṣàngó through his devotees and emblems. Here he follows modern Nigerian artists, such as Ben Enwonwu, who portray him in person.
      • Ọbalúayé’s face stays hidden under straw, as it does in Candomblé.
      • These are living religions in Nigeria and across the Americas. Their priests and practitioners are the authorities on their own gods.
