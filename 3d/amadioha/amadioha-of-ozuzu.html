What is sourced, what is imagined

Wikipedia gives two pictures of Amadioha that cannot both be literal: a fair-skinned, titled gentleman of cool temper, and no physical form at all save a gigantic white ram. This study shows both, side by side. The list keeps what the sources say apart from what was designed.

From the sources

  • A fair-skinned, titled gentleman of cool temper, and a gigantic white ram as his only physical form. Wikipedia: Amadioha
  • Red as his colour, and white as his favourite, so a white ram is the sacrifice he prefers. Red cap and isiagụ, white wrapper and ram. Wikipedia, Encyclopedia.com
  • Mmaagha Kamalu, a sword believed to glow red near people with evil intentions. Wikipedia: Amadioha
  • The isiagụ: leopard head; usually red; long or short sleeves; gold buttons linked by a chain; a breast pocket; knee-length. Wikipedia: Isiagu, UmuIgbo
  • Okpu mme, the red cap of communities with Nze na Ọzọ title-holders, and the eagle feather Ọzọ men affix to it. UmuIgbo, Ozikoro, My Igbo Name
  • Coral beads among Igbo jewellery. UmuIgbo
  • The akupe, and fans of animal skin carried by chiefs. Love Weddings NG
  • The ram's breed: often all white; rams horned, at most one and a half coils; a well-developed throat ruff; kept widely in southern Nigeria. Wikipedia: West African Dwarf sheep, Nigerian Finder

This study's choices

  • His face, age and beard. The lighter skin follows "fair-skinned"; the age, the build and the grey in the beard are ours.
  • The leopard-head print. An original motif in a half-drop repeat, with gold embroidery at the neck and down the placket.
  • The chain's route, from the top button across to the pocket.
  • Three feathers, white with dark tips, at the left side of the cap. The sources give neither number nor colour.
  • The sword's form: its leaf-shaped blade, incised lines, brass fittings and leather grip, and how the glow looks.
  • The fan's round shape, burned zigzag and red binding.
  • The white wrapper, pairing his two colours, as in the gallery figure.
  • The ivory bangle and coral bracelet, worn as decoration, not as attributes.
  • The ram's size and pose, and the setting: a night storm over damp red laterite, lightning far off.

How they were made

The body starts from MakeHuman's free base mesh, shaped older and heavier than Ṣàngó's, rigged and posed bone by bone. The right fist was closed round the sword's grip with the wrist turned so the blade points down and out; the left holds the fan upright.

The wrapper is one sheet wrapped 1.2 times round the waist, its top pinned and the rest left to fall. The isiagụ is a single T-shaped pattern folded over the shoulders, its sides and sleeves sewn and its hem vented. It was draped with the arms out, then simulated while the arms moved into the pose, with the wrapper under it as a solid.

The ram is 55 overlapping body shapes blended into one surface. Its horns are swept along a tightening spiral with growth ridges, and its coat is grown hair by hair, lengthening and gathering into locks in the throat ruff.
