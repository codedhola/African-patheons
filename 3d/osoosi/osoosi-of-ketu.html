The hunter of one arrow

A myth told in the candomblé houses of Brazil, as Pierre Verger recorded it. Olon, king of Ifẹ̀, was keeping the festival of the new yams when a huge bird flew over the feast and settled on the roof of his palace. The witches had sent it, angry that they had not been invited.

Three hunters tried and missed, and were thrown in prison. The fourth came from Iremã with a single arrow. His mother, told by a diviner that her only son stood a step from death or from riches, opened the breast of a hen and laid it on the road for the witches. The bird reached for the offering, its charm slipped, and the one arrow struck it full in the breast.

The king gave the hunter half his fortune; the freed hunters gave him sacks of cowries; and the diviner sang Oxowusi!, “the hunter Oxo is popular”, which became his name. This figure is drawn at the moment before the arrow leaves the string.

After J. M. Souza, Revista Mosaico (2020), retelling Verger’s Lendas africanas dos orixás (1997)

What is sourced, what is imagined

None of the sources used here describes how Ọ̀ṣọ́ọ̀sì looks. What he wears and carries follows his gallery figure and what the sources say of his colours and symbols, so this figure is an interpretation, not a cult image. This list keeps the two apart.

From the sources

  • A hunter and warrior, one of the most important of Yorùbá culture, worshipped in Nigeria, Benin and Togo. Wikipedia: Oshosi
  • The bow and arrow, ọrun àti ọfà, his main symbols; in Candomblé he hunts with the ofá. Wikipedia: Oshosi
  • Kétu: on the Benin side of Yorùbáland he is called Ọ̀ṣọ́ọ̀sìn; Souza calls him a hunter and king of Ketu. Wikipedia: Oshosi, Souza
  • Blue: his colour in the Ketu nation of Candomblé, his ritual garment blue and his necklace blue beads. Wikipedia: Oshosi
  • The forest, its animals and wealth are his in Candomblé, and guinea fowl are among what is offered to him. Wikipedia: Oshosi
  • The hunter of one arrow, who killed the witches' bird at the king's yam festival and was paid in half the king's fortune and in cowries. Souza, after Verger
  • The iroko, held by the Yorùbá to house a spirit, Olúwéré, and revered with offerings where it grows. Wikipedia: Iroko

This study’s choices

  • His face, age and build. None of the sources used here describes how he looks.
  • The moment: full draw, his one arrow aimed up into the trees. What he aims at is left out of the picture.
  • Following his gallery figure: the blue tunic over short trousers, three strands of blue beads, the quiver on his back on a strap over his right shoulder.
  • The cloth: indigo-dyed strips, pale sky blue with deep indigo stripes for the tunic and deep indigo for the trousers; the stripes are this study's.
  • The bow, arrows and quiver: their forms and woods, the leaf-shaped iron heads, and the guinea-fowl fletching, chosen for the bird offered to him.
  • The draw: three fingers on the string, the arrow between the first two, anchored at the corner of the jaw.
  • The forest: a clearing after stage 06 of this repo, with its iroko, offerings, ferns, lianas, fallen log and blue-green mist.
  • Where sources differ. In Candomblé his colour is blue in the Ketu nation and green elsewhere; this figure, of Kétu, wears blue.

How he was made

The body starts from MakeHuman’s free base mesh, shaped as a lean young hunter and rigged. An archer shooting upward bends at the waist to keep the T of his shoulders and arms, so his upper body is tilted along the shot. The drawing hand was placed first: forty-five places for the nock were tried round the corner of his jaw, and the one kept is where his hooked fingers come closest to his face without pressing into it. The bow’s grip is a full draw of 66 cm ahead of it along the shot, and the bow arm is solved to reach it.

The bow is built already bent, each limb an arc bent as far as a string of fixed length lets it come, and his left hand closes on its leather grip finger by finger. The string runs through the crooks of his three hooked fingers, and the arrow rests on his fist, raised until it clears the skin.

The tunic and the drawstring trousers are cut from his body and simulated. The sleeves are cut round his arms close to where they end, since a sleeve carried far up with a rising arm is left behind inside it. The strap is his body’s section in a plane through his right shoulder and his left side, pulled taut, and the quiver lies along its run across his back. The forest is Ọ̀ṣun’s grove without the river, with the iroko larger than any tree round it; its canopy of nearly a quarter of a million leaves is stored as one leaf and the places of the rest.

Every stage is a Python script in 3d/osoosi/blender/, using the toolkit in 3d/toolkit/, and the finished scene is 3d/osoosi/osoosi.blend.
