# Fighting stages: Yorùbá and Igbo environments

Twenty-five 2D backgrounds for a Mortal Kombat-style fighting game: 15 Yorùbá and 10 Igbo. They are flat cel-shaded SVG boards on the Design canvas "Yorùbá Environment Sketch", in the "Yorùbá fighting stages" rows and the two "Igbo fighting stages" rows: https://claude.ai/artifact/9fnST2yrYp2ZZfvdAupU6z

## Shared stage spec

- Each plate is 1600 × 900 px. The walkable floor starts between y 650 and y 700.
- Fighters' feet sit on a ground line at y 772. Start positions are x 478 (P1) and x 1134 (P2), with fighters about 400 px tall, as in the Ṣàngó vs Ògún scene.
- The top 150 px stays calm for the HUD.
- Framing pieces stay at the edges, outside x 300 to x 1300 at floor level.
- Each board has a "showGuides" toggle. It overlays the ground line, the HUD band and two fighter silhouettes.

| 04 | Òkè Ìdànrè | Inselbergs about 3,000 ft high. People lived on the boulders for almost a millennium and moved downhill in 1923. Owa's palace, shrines and burial mounds remain | UNESCO tentative list |
| 05 | Savanna bush path | Woodland savanna in the north of Yorubaland | Wikipedia: Yorubaland |
| 06 | High forest | The iroko is believed to hold a spirit (Olúwéré) and is sometimes revered with offerings | Wikipedia: Iroko |
| 07 | Night market | Markets ran daily, at night, and on 4- or 8-day cycles; women ran the local markets | EveryCulture |
| 08 | Ògún's forge | Metalworking was men's work | EveryCulture |
| 09 | Adire dye yard | Indigo adire is made by Yoruba women using oniko (tied), alabere (stitched) and eleko (paste) resists; Abẹ́òkúta is its centre. The city takes its name from Olúmọ Rock ("under the rock") | Wikipedia: Adire; Showcaves |
| 10 | Olúmirin Falls | The falls have seven levels, with the village of Abake on top | Wikipedia: Erin-Ijesha Waterfalls |
| 11 | Ṣàngó's storm hill | The odó Ṣàngó is a mortar-shaped altar. It holds edun ara (stone axes Ṣàngó hurls as thunderbolts) and carries double-axe, dog and zigzag-snake reliefs | Tribal Art |
| 12 | Ẹrẹdọ ditch | The ditch runs over 160 km and is up to about 20 m deep from its floor to the bank rim. It is cut in laterite, now a "green tunnel", and linked by legend to Bilikisu Sungbo | Wikipedia: Sungbo's Eredo |
| 13 | Ruins of Old Ọ̀yọ́ | Abandoned since 1835, with walls surviving in woodland savanna; now a national park | Wikipedia: Old Oyo |

| 15 | The Niger, Ọya's river | Ọya is patroness of the Niger (Odò-Ọya) and òrìṣà of winds, lightning and storms. Her symbols are the buffalo and red, purple and burgundy, and she is called "mother of nine" | Wikipedia: Oya |

## Igbo stages: what each draws on

| # | Stage | Research basis | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | Mbari house of Ala | An offering to Ala, the earth goddess, from the Owerri area. It is a temporary, open-sided square shelter of anthill clay with a thatched roof that extends past its corners. Inside are life-sized, brightly painted clay figures with exaggerated heads and necks and long limbs: deities such as Ala and Amadioha, Europeans, animals, craftsmen and legendary beasts. The house is left to decay back into the earth | Wikipedia: Mbari house |
| 17 | Ogbunike Caves | The entrance opens into a chamber about 5 m high, 10 m wide and 30 m long, with ten tunnels leading off. Bats live in the caves, and streams run through them, one into the River Nkisa; there is a waterfall in the northwest part. Visitors remove their shoes, the Ime Ogba festival marks the caves' discovery, and rainforest surrounds the site | Wikipedia: Ogbunike Caves; UNESCO tentative list |
| 18 | Oguta Lake | The largest natural lake in Imo State, in equatorial rainforest; Uhamiri is the goddess of the lake. Canoes carry goods and people, and women ferment cassava by the canoes. A popular account says the green lake water (Ogbuide) and the brown river water (Urashi) meet without mixing, with a shrine on either side | Wikipedia: Oguta Lake; Face2Face Africa |
| 19 | Family compound and obi | Compounds are built around a central courtyard inside mud walls, with carved hardwood posts and doors. In Achebe's *Things Fall Apart*, the obi stands behind the only gate, the wives' huts form a half moon behind it, and there is a barn of yams and a medicine house. Uli wall paintings are made almost only by women, in white clay, yellow earth, red-brown camwood and black charcoal. Motifs include swelling and tapering curves, leopard spots and python markings, divided by rows of white dots (ntupo). Yam barns tie each tuber to an upright frame of poles | Wikipedia: Igbo architecture; Uli; Glendora Review essay; FAO |
| 20 | Village square, ikoro night | The ikoro is an enormous slit drum cut from a log. It is kept in a fixed place, usually the village square, and beaten with sticks; when it sounds, people gather at the square. Mmanwụ masquerades represent spirits and ancestors and are performed only by men; women watch from a distance, and some masquerades come out at night | Wikipedia: Ikoro; Mmanwu |
| 21 | Ofala, the palace arena | Onitsha's Ofala, mostly in October, ends the Obi's week-long seclusion. It opens with a 21-gun salute and all-night Ufie royal drumming. Red-cap chiefs (Ndichie) arrive in procession at the palace grounds (Ime Obi) to royal music (Egwu Ota). A cannon shot announces the Obi, who appears in a ceremonial robe with a bronze sword and dances in the arena on his second appearance. Tens of thousands gather, and the chiefs kneel and dance by seniority | Wikipedia: Ofala Festival; NICO |
| 22 | Udi Hills escarpment | The Udi–Nsukka Plateau is mostly sandstone, with steep escarpments, an average height a little over 350 m and a highest point of 592 m. It has tropical savanna, and streams cascade over the eastern escarpment. The Udi Hills are ringed with granite and sandstone boulders, and the summit looks out over Enugu, where coal was found in 1909 | Wikipedia: Udi-Nsukka Plateau; Udi Hills |
| 23 | Idemili river, the python's home | Idemili takes its name from the Idemili River, held to be a goddess who sent the royal python (Eke Idemili) as her messenger. The python is revered and must not be harmed or eaten; one found in a home is gently guided out, and one killed by mistake is given burial rites like a person | Idemili North LGA; The Sun |
| 24 | Raffia swamp before dawn | Tappers (onye ngwọ for raffia, onye nkwu for oil palm) start before dawn and climb with a belt called akpaka, sometimes over 30 ft. At the crown they trim a flower stalk so the sap drips into a gourd; the wine is kept in calabashes or clay pots. Palm wine is used in marriage rites, the New Yam festival and libations. Raffia tappers may reach their trees by canoe through rainforest | Oriire; Free Malaysia Today (AFP) |
| 25 | New Yam festival | Held at the end of the rainy season, in early August. Yams are offered to the gods and ancestors first, then the oldest man, the king or a titled elder eats the first yam, roasted and served with palm oil. Old yams are finished or thrown away on the eve. The day brings folk dances, masquerades and parades | Wikipedia: New Yam Festival of the Igbo; Oriire |

## Design choices (not from sources)

- All lighting, times of day, palettes and compositions.
- The sculptures in 01 are original shapes, not copies of the grove's artworks.
- In 05: the harmattan haze, termite mounds and the cowrie shrine at the crossroads. In 06: the glowing eyes between the iroko's roots.
- In 08, the palm-frond fringe and the dog follow the Ògún imagery used in the earlier gallery.
- In 12: the log bridge and the guard on the rim.
- The buffalo-horn post with nine cloth strips in 15 combines Ọya's symbols. It is not a documented shrine form.
- The Mbari figures in 16 are original, stylised figures. Their arrangement on two clay tiers, the painted posts and the termite mound are composition choices.
- In 18, the two shrine huts and their flags are invented, and the seam between the waters is stylised.
- The compound in 19 is a composite of the sources, not one real compound.
- The masquerade costumes in 20 and 25 are original designs, not specific named masquerades. The big tree and the bonfire in 20 are composition choices.
- In 21 the throne stands empty, just before the Obi emerges; no real person is depicted. The palace building, its thatch and the throne's canopy are generic, not the actual palace.
- In 22, the mesa with the waterfall and the city lights far below are composition choices.
- In 23, the small shrine on the bank is invented; the sources describe no shrine.
- In 24, the tapper's lamp, the fire and the props are composition choices.
- In 25, the strings of young palm fronds, the yam heap on a mat and the elder's house are composition choices.
